# DIE BREMER STADTMUSIKANTEN

**Based on:** “The Bremen Town Musicians” by the Brothers Grimm  
**English title:** The Bremen Town Musicians  
**Film language:** German (spoken dialogue)  
**Intended audience:** Ages 5–8  
**Target runtime:** Approximately 7 minutes  
**Adaptation note:** The danger that sends each animal away is clear, but no one is shown being harmed. The robbers' defeat is played as a comic fright and escape.

## Logline

Four aging animals who have been cast aside join forces on the road to Bremen, discover that their voices make a powerful band, and find a safe home by driving a group of robbers from an empty house.

## Characters

- **DONKEY** — Tired from years of work, steady and practical. He takes the lead and makes room for others.
- **HOUND** — An old hunting dog, loyal and quick to react to sounds. He worries that he has nothing left to offer.
- **CAT** — An older house cat, observant and self-possessed. She values warmth and comfort, but is brave when her friends need her.
- **ROOSTER** — A loud, watchful farm rooster. He notices distant activity before the others and is proud of his voice.
- **CAPTAIN** — The leader of the robbers. Confident while surrounded by his companions, easily shaken by the animals' unexpected entrance.
- **ROBBERS** — The Captain's companions. They flee the house and do not return.

## Screenplay

### SCENE 1 — THE ROAD OUT
**Time:** 00:00–00:45 | **Duration:** 45 seconds  
**Setting:** A mill yard opening onto a country road. Loaded sacks are stacked near the mill door. The road begins just beyond a simple gate; once Donkey passes through it, the mill is behind him.

**Action and direction:**  
Donkey carries one last sack toward the mill. His steps slow. He lowers the sack carefully, pauses to catch his breath, and tries to lift it again. His legs tremble; the sack slips back to the ground.

From inside the mill, his MASTER speaks to someone out of sight. The words are indistinct, but Donkey understands that the miller no longer wants to keep him. Donkey looks toward the door. He does not wait to hear more. He backs away, turns, and walks through the gate.

At the road, he stops and looks toward the distant direction of Bremen. He straightens as much as his tired body allows and starts forward with a firmer step.

**Dialogue:**

**DONKEY**  
Ich habe jahrelang Säcke getragen. Bis Bremen schaffe ich es noch aus eigener Kraft.

He continues down the road.

---

### SCENE 2 — THE TIRED HOUND
**Time:** 00:45–01:30 | **Duration:** 45 seconds  
**Setting:** A country road with a grassy verge and a shallow ditch. The mill is no longer visible. The road continues in the same direction.

**Action and direction:**  
Donkey hears fast, uneven breathing ahead. Hound lies beside the road, trying to rise. He gets his front paws under him, but his back legs give way and he settles down again.

Donkey stops at a respectful distance, then lowers his head so Hound can see him. Hound tenses at first. Donkey waits without crowding him.

**Dialogue:**

**DONKEY**  
Was lässt dich so keuchen, alter Freund?

**HOUND**  
Ich kann nicht mehr so schnell laufen. Mein Herr sagt, für die Jagd tauge ich nicht mehr. Ich weiß nicht, wohin ich soll.

Donkey shifts his weight, testing his tired legs, then offers Hound his shoulder.

**DONKEY**  
Ich gehe nach Bremen, um Stadtmusikant zu werden. Ich spiele Laute.

Hound glances at Donkey's empty hooves.

**HOUND**  
Wo ist deine Laute?

**DONKEY**  
Noch habe ich keine gefunden. Aber du kannst den Takt schlagen. Komm mit.

Hound rises again, this time using Donkey's shoulder for balance. They set off side by side. Hound's pace is slower, so Donkey shortens his stride to match it.

---

### SCENE 3 — THE CAT BY THE LANE
**Time:** 01:30–02:10 | **Duration:** 40 seconds  
**Setting:** A narrow lane beside a cottage and its garden hedge. A low stone step sits near the road. The lane joins the road ahead.

**Action and direction:**  
Donkey and Hound approach the cottage. Cat sits on the step with her tail wrapped around her paws. She watches them pass without moving.

Hound notices her and slows. Donkey turns back. Cat's ears angle toward them, but she keeps her comfortable position.

**Dialogue:**

**DONKEY**  
Was bedrückt dich, alte Samtpfote?

**CAT**  
Meine Zähne sind nicht mehr, was sie einmal waren. Ich sitze lieber am warmen Ofen, als Mäuse zu jagen. Meine Menschen sagen, sie brauchen mich nicht mehr.

Cat looks toward the cottage door, then away from it. Donkey offers the same invitation he gave Hound.

**DONKEY**  
Komm mit nach Bremen. Du kennst dich mit Nachtmusik aus.

**CAT**  
Ein Liedchen kenne ich schon.

**HOUND**  
Und ich halte den Takt.

Cat rises, stretches each front paw, and steps down from the stone. She joins them, taking the middle of the road so she can walk between her new companions.

---

### SCENE 4 — THE ROOSTER'S WARNING
**Time:** 02:10–02:55 | **Duration:** 45 seconds  
**Setting:** A farm entrance with a gate, a yard, and a crossbeam above the gate. The road runs along the outside of the fence.

**Action and direction:**  
A loud crow rings out from above. The three travelers stop. Rooster stands on the crossbeam, calling again and again.

Donkey steps closer to the gate. Hound stands beside him, alert but calm. Cat remains half a step behind them and looks from Rooster to the yard.

**Dialogue:**

**DONKEY**  
Du hast eine kräftige Stimme. Wovor warnst du alle?

**ROOSTER**  
Der Koch will mich morgen beim Sonntagsessen auf den Tisch bringen. Da singe ich lieber, solange ich noch kann.

Rooster's call falters. He looks down at the three animals waiting below.

**DONKEY**  
Komm mit nach Bremen. Wir brauchen einen Sänger.

**ROOSTER**  
Ist in eurer Band auch Platz für einen Hahn?

**CAT**  
Neben mir ist Platz.

Rooster hops down from the beam to the fence rail, then to the ground. He lands, regains his balance, and joins the group. The four turn toward the road together.

---

### SCENE 5 — A LIGHT IN THE WOODS
**Time:** 02:55–03:45 | **Duration:** 50 seconds  
**Setting:** A forest path leading into a small clearing. A broad tree stands near the path. Far beyond the trees, a house light can be seen through the branches.

**Action and direction:**  
The four travel along the path as evening falls. Donkey leads; Hound follows close behind. Cat walks along the edge of the path, stopping briefly to check the undergrowth. Rooster keeps to the safest low branches and returns to the group whenever the path opens.

When the light fades, they stop beneath the broad tree. Donkey and Hound settle at its base. Cat climbs to a low branch. Rooster climbs higher and looks out in each direction.

Rooster spots a small light in the distance. He leans forward and calls down. The others stand and look where he points. The light is far away, but steady.

**Dialogue:**

**ROOSTER**  
Da! Ein Haus, ganz in der Nähe!

**DONKEY**  
Dann suchen wir dort ein Nachtlager. Unter diesem Baum können wir nicht bleiben.

**HOUND**  
Vielleicht gibt es dort auch etwas zu essen.

Hound's stomach growls. Cat looks at him, then toward the light.

**CAT**  
Finden wir's heraus.

They leave the tree and continue toward the house, staying together on the path.

---

### SCENE 6 — THE HOUSE IN THE WOODS
**Time:** 03:45–04:35 | **Duration:** 50 seconds  
**Setting:** The outside of a house in the forest. One window faces the path. Through it, the animals can see a main room, a table set with food, and several robbers gathered around it. The window can be opened from outside.

**Action and direction:**  
As the animals approach, the light grows stronger. They hear voices and stop before reaching the window.

Donkey moves to the wall beneath the window and rises on his hind legs just enough to see into the room. Hound waits beside him, watching the door. Cat circles to the other side of the window to get a clearer view. Rooster stays behind the group, looking and listening.

Donkey lowers himself and reports what he sees: a table full of food and robbers eating. The others gather close, but keep low beneath the window.

**Dialogue:**

**ROOSTER**  
Das sieht nach einem Festmahl aus.

**HOUND**  
Und genug für alle.

**DONKEY**  
Wir müssen sie vertreiben. Lasst mich nachdenken.

Donkey studies the window ledge, then the others. He demonstrates the order with small nods: himself at the bottom, Hound on his back, Cat on Hound, Rooster on Cat.

**CAT**  
Wir sollen aufeinander klettern?

**DONKEY**  
Nur so lange, bis wir einen ganz besonderen Auftritt hinlegen.

Hound steps close behind Donkey. Cat places her paws on Hound's shoulders and tests her balance. Rooster waits until Cat is steady, then prepares to perch above her. Donkey positions himself beneath the openable window.

---

### SCENE 7 — THE BAND'S FIRST CONCERT
**Time:** 04:35–05:45 | **Duration:** 70 seconds  
**Setting:** The main room of the house. The table is at the center, with food and cups on it. The window is on one side of the room; the main door leads outside. The robbers are gathered around the table.

**Action and direction:**  
From outside, the animals complete their stack. Donkey braces his front hooves on the low window ledge. Hound climbs carefully onto Donkey's back and steadies himself. Cat steps from Hound's shoulders to the top of the stack. Rooster settles above Cat, keeping his balance with small foot adjustments.

They pause. Donkey looks up to check that everyone is ready. Each animal gives a small nod or answer. Donkey stamps once.

Together, they make their loudest music: Donkey brays, Hound barks, Cat yowls, and Rooster crows. On the final beat, they push through the open window into the room as one tumbling group.

The robbers spring up from the table. One knocks over a chair while backing away; another bumps the door open. The Captain calls for everyone to run. The robbers rush out into the woods, leaving the food behind.

The animals untangle themselves and check that all four are present. Hound looks at Donkey; Cat helps Rooster down from the stack. Donkey tests the room before inviting everyone to the table.

**Dialogue:**

**DONKEY**  
Seid ihr alle heil?

**ROOSTER**  
Und noch ganz bei Stimme!

The four move to the table. Donkey leaves room for Hound to climb onto a chair. Cat chooses the warmest nearby place. Rooster takes a spot where he can see the door. They share the remaining food, passing it around rather than crowding one another.

**HOUND**  
Das war wohl unser erstes Konzert.

**CAT**  
Unser Publikum ist aber früh gegangen.

They laugh. The four continue eating as the room grows quiet.

---

### SCENE 8 — THE MIDNIGHT VISITOR
**Time:** 05:45–07:00 | **Duration:** 75 seconds  
**Setting:** The same house, later that night. The table has been cleared enough for the animals to rest. The hearth is at one side of the room, the outside door is nearby, and a crossbeam runs above the room.

**Action and direction:**  
The animals settle according to their habits. Donkey lies in a dry patch of straw near the wall. Hound curls beside the door. Cat rests by the warm hearth. Rooster climbs to the crossbeam. Each checks that the others are settled before closing their eyes.

After midnight, the Captain watches from outside. Seeing no light and hearing no sound, he sends one robber back to check the house. The robber enters slowly, keeping one hand on the door and listening.

The robber sees Cat's eyes reflecting near the hearth and mistakes them for glowing coals. He reaches toward the hearth with a small taper. Cat springs away from the hearth, lands in front of him, and swipes his cap off. The robber yelps and backs toward the door.

Hound rises and barks sharply from the doorway. The robber jumps sideways to avoid him. Donkey wakes and gives a quick kick that sends a wooden stool skidding across the floor in front of the robber. He stumbles over the stool without falling.

Rooster, startled awake by the noise, crows from the beam. The robber looks up, then rushes out through the door.

**Dialogue:**

**ROBBER**  
In dem Haus wimmelt es von Ungeheuern! Eine Hexe am Herd hat mir die Mütze vom Kopf gerissen! Ein Wächter an der Tür hat nach meinem Bein geschnappt! Ein schwarzes Ungetüm hat mir einen Hocker vor die Füße gekickt! Und oben sitzt ein Richter, der nach mir ruft!

The robber runs back to the Captain. We hear the Captain's alarmed reply, then hurried footsteps retreating into the woods.

The animals listen until the house is quiet again. Hound returns to his place by the door. Cat settles back by the hearth. Donkey lowers his head onto the straw. Rooster remains awake for a moment, watching over them.

**ROOSTER**  
Bremen kann bis morgen warten.

**DONKEY**  
Einverstanden.

**HOUND**  
Nach dem Frühstück.

**CAT**  
Und nach einem Nickerchen.

Rooster gives one softer crow. The others answer with a bray, a bark, and a purr-like meow: a small, sleepy version of their band. They settle into the house together.
